### How tokens work

Tokens are a flexible deadline extension system. You allocate tokens to students at the start of the term, and they spend them to buy extra hours on an assignment, before or after its deadline. No emails, no individual negotiations.

### Setting the token cost

Go to **Settings > Extension** and set **Tokens per hour**: how many tokens it costs a student to extend a deadline by one hour. It applies to every assignment that doesn't set its own price, and changing it reprices those assignments right away.

To price one assignment differently, set **Tokens per extension hour** on that assignment. Leave the field empty to use the classroom's price, or set it to 0 to turn extensions off for that assignment.

### Assigning tokens to students

Go to **Tokens** and click **Assign Tokens**. You can assign to specific students or to everyone at once. Add an optional description like "start of semester allocation" to keep the history readable. Tokens are added to each student's balance immediately. Do this before anyone needs them, it takes 10 seconds and saves a lot of awkward conversations later.

### How students use tokens

Students see an **Extend** button on each priced repository assignment on their **Assignments** page, and can use it at any time: ahead of the deadline, while the work is late, or after a late submission. They pick how many hours they want, see the token cost (hours x tokens per hour for that assignment), and confirm. Their balance is debited and that many hours past the deadline no longer count as late, so the late penalty shrinks accordingly. The assignment's deadline itself doesn't change; students see a note under it saying how many hours they applied. A student can cancel a purchase from their Tokens page to get the tokens back, which takes the hours back too.

### Token history

The Tokens page shows a full log of every transaction across the classroom: who received tokens, who spent them, and when. Students can see their own transaction history on their Tokens page. Everything is logged — you'll never have to take anyone's word for it.
